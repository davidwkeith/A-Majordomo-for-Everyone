A rotary telephone handset and a modern smartphone in 8-bit pixel
art, approximately 56x40 pixels scaled up, resting side by side on
an implied flat surface (no surface drawn, just the two objects).
The handset is rendered with the curved weight of a 1970s Bell-style
receiver, coiled cord trailing off and cut at the frame edge. The
smartphone is a plain dark rectangle with a single screen-glow pixel
row, no visible icons or UI. Palette: Black for the receiver body and
phone bezel, Charcoal for shading, Blue for the phone's screen glow,
Recycled for the receiver's highlight edge. Colored in ballpoint pen
and pencil per the inline-graphics style.

**True pixel grid --- non-negotiable.** Actual 8-bit pixel art, not a
blocky illustration. Render on a literal uniform grid of axis-aligned
square pixels, as if captured from an NES framebuffer and enlarged
with nearest-neighbor scaling. Every pixel the same size, hard edges
between colors, no anti-aliasing, no smooth curves, no sub-pixel
detail. The viewer should be able to count pixels along an edge.

**No meta-elements --- non-negotiable.** Only the two phones. No
color swatches, palettes, legends, keys, hex codes, callouts, arrows,
sidebar text, or any UI explaining the colors or technique. No
caption text inside the image.

**Background: pure white, `#FFFFFF`, flat.** Not gray, not off-white,
not cream, not paper texture. The build removes white to create
transparency, so any gray will show as a halo in the ePub.

**Watch out for:**

- NO hand or figure holding either phone --- object-focused
- NO readable text, icons, or app UI on the smartphone screen, just
  a flat glow color
- NO cable connecting the two objects --- they are simply adjacent
- NO speech bubble, thought bubble, or dialogue text
