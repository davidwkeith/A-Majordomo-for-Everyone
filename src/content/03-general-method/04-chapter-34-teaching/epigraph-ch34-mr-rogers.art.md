Mr. Rogers in 8-bit pixel art, approximately 32x48 pixels scaled up.
Kneeling on one knee rather than standing --- getting down to eye
level with someone just outside the frame. Red cardigan sweater,
gentle attentive posture, hands open rather than gesturing. 4-color
palette drawn from the master palette: Red for the cardigan, Skin
Light for face and hands, Warm Gray for hair, Charcoal for pants and
shoes. Colored in ballpoint pen over a pencil grid.

**True pixel grid --- non-negotiable.** This is actual 8-bit pixel
art, not a blocky illustration. Render on a literal uniform grid of
axis-aligned square pixels, as if captured from an NES framebuffer
and enlarged with nearest-neighbor scaling. Every pixel the same
size, every pixel a single flat color from the palette. Hard edges
between colors, no anti-aliasing, no smooth curves, no airbrush, no
sub-pixel detail. Diagonals are stair-stepped. The viewer should be
able to count pixels along an edge. Reference: Mega Man, Final
Fantasy IV, Link's Awakening --- not a modern illustrator's "retro"
style.

**No meta-elements --- non-negotiable.** The illustration contains
only the figure. No color swatches, palettes, legends, keys, hex
codes, callouts, arrows, sidebar text, or any UI explaining the
colors or technique. No caption text inside the image (no "Mr.
Rogers," no "Neighborhood," no name label).

**Background: pure white, `#FFFFFF`, flat.** Not gray, not off-white,
not cream, not paper texture, not a notebook page. The build removes
white to create transparency, so any gray will show as a halo in the
ePub.

**Watch out for:**

- NO trolley, no puppet, no "Neighborhood of Make-Believe" set
  pieces --- the figure stands alone
- NO speech bubble, thought bubble, or dialogue text
- NO zipper/cardigan-change sequence --- a single kneeling pose, not
  a filmstrip
- NO other figures visible in frame --- the person he's speaking to
  is implied by his posture and gaze, not drawn
